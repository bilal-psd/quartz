# WW1

## League of Nations

![[_attachments/Untitled 2 5.png|Untitled 2 5.png]]

- The most significant consequence of WW1, and especially with important implications in West Asia
- Global ambition to prevent wars
- Brainchild of US President **Woodrow Wilson**
- **Security dilemma** - country A takes action to defend itself → invokes perception that this is offensive in nature → country B takes action → A and B try to match either →→ both countries are **no more secure** than the point where they started
- To counter security dilemma → **Collective security:**
  - An aggressor against any one state is considered an aggressor against all other states
  - They act together to repel the aggressor
  - _“All for one and one for all”_
- As far as the mandate to prevent wars are concerned → failure → it failed to prevent WWII

  ![[_attachments/1520223638343.jpeg]]

  - US was not a member state
    - Political reason: Foreign policy oscillates between two extremes:
      - Isolationism: avoid alliances with other nations to prevent being drawn into wars not related to direct territorial self-defense
      - Engagement / Intervention
    - Constitutional reason: President’s agreement with another country has to be ratified by the Senate and Congress
      - Senate did not ratify
  - Russians were not invited
  - Any organisation in the absence of the dominant players will be a weak organisation
- **Mandatory regime**

### 1. **McMahon–Hussein Correspondence**

- Between British and Arabs (1915-16)
  - Hussein ibn Ali, emir (grand sharif) of Mecca
    - Hashemite clan (current royal family Jordan)
  - Sir Henry McMahon, the British high commissioner in Egypt
    - Man behind India-Tibet border
- On what would be the political fate of the territory
- Rebel against the Ottoman Empire and in return, the entire territory of Greater Syria would be given sovereignty

### 2. **Sykes-Picot Agreement (1916)**

![[_attachments/Untitled 4 3.png|Untitled 4 3.png]]

- Secret Agreement
- Between British and France → later Russia also entered
- how to divide the Ottoman territories of West Asia → first thing was to divide Greater Syria
  - France → Syria and Lebanon
  - Britain → Palestine and Iraq
  - Russia →Turkish strait (connects black sea and Mediterranean sea) - very strategically important strait for the Russians

### 3. **Balfour declaration (1917)**

![[_attachments/3937.jpg.webp]]

- Arthur Balfour (British Foreign Secretary) to Lord Rothschild (leader of Zionist federation)
- “Land without people for people without land” - Zionist idea
- International travellers like Mark Twain came to Palestine during the phase of the agricultural cycle when there was no one there
- Declaration did not declare that the majority (90%) of the population were Arabs → termed “non-Jewish” communities
- Only civil and religious rights protected → not **National** rights
- Tyrannical: British who **never owned** this land were promising this land to the Zionists who **didn’t have any claim** for the land; **without the consent** of the people living there
- Two clauses:
  - Enabling clause: "establishment of a national home for the Jewish...”→ actualisation of Jewish homeland
  - Limiting clause: nothing shall be done which may prejudice the civil and religious rights of the existing communities
- Non-reconciliation of these two clauses

# Miscellaneous

## Confederation vs Federation

### Confederation

- In a confederation, states come together creating a loose (often temporary) union for matters of political, economic or administrative convenience.
- Within a confederation, member states **maintain their sovereignty** and often appoint a weak central authority to speed up bureaucratic matters.

### Federation

- States or provinces that join a federation, agree to **give up part of their powers** and to answer to the central government, which has the power to enforce laws and regulations.
