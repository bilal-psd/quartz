# Camp David accords

![[_attachments/image 5.png|image 5.png]]

> Only Arab-Israel peace treaty that still holds

## Background

1. Consequence of Yom Kippur war - 1973
   - Saadat wanted to break the status quo
2. Saadat did something that shook the world → he was ready to negotiate with the Israelis → first Arab leader to do that
   - There was on overwhelming idea that Begin would never return the favour
   - Even more shockingly → Begin invited Sadat to Israel
3. US President Jimmy Carter invited Begin and Sadat to Camp David

   ![[_attachments/image 14.jpeg|image 14.jpeg]]

   - Camp David → presidential retreat of US presidents
   - Carter played a good role as mediator
     - facilitated these negotiations
   - 13 days of negotiation
   - Entire negotiations was kept away from the scrutiny of the media
     - No media was allowed to cover the negotiations
     - Whatever we know is from the accounts of the people who were actually there
     - If media was allowed to cover → parties to the conflict would take resort to no-compromise positions
       - But if the media is removed → parties will be more comfortable to begin even talking about the idea of compromise

## Structure

Two framework agreements

1. Framework for Peace in the Middle East
2. Framework Peace Treaty Egypt and Israel
   - Led to the Egypt-Israel peace treaty

## Consequence

- As a result of the treaty → Israel withdrew from Sinai peninsula and gave it back to Egypt
  - Both the leaders received Nobel Peace Prize
  - Israel argument → By doing this, they fulfilled their obligation as required by UN resolution 242
- For this Egypt was banished from Arab League
- Saadat was assassinated in 1981
- Why did Israel agree to hand over Sinai to Egypt but not the territories to Syria and Jordan?
  - Calculated risk → Neutralise Egypt
    - Egypt is the most important country in the region; through all the wars
    - Any alliance without Egypt will be a weak alliance

# Madrid peace conference -1991

## Background

- End of Cold War was heralded by a war in West Asia
- First Gulf War / Persian Gulf War
  - Iraq invasion and occupation of Kuwait
    - US → first instance of humanitarian intervention
  - Saddam Hussein led
- US banded together an alliance → successfully dislodged Iraq from Kuwait

![[_attachments/image 1 2.png|image 1 2.png]]

### Humanitarian Intervention

- Other states can intervene in instances of genocide etc
- Controversial → comes in the way of the two pillars of world politics:
  - Non-intervention
  - Not using force: Only two instances in which use of force is legitimate
    - self defence
    - using force is sanctioned by Security Council

## Structure

- Madrid, Washington, Moscow
- Involved both bilateral and multilateral
- Negotiations took place in front of the media
  - When under the glaring lens of the media, making compromise is difficult

![[_attachments/image 1 2.jpeg|image 1 2.jpeg]]

## Significance

- Limited success
  - Attributed to the presence of media
- First time all the parties of the conflict had come to negotiate face to face
- Israel → ‘official policy’ to not negotiate with terrorists i.e. PLO
  - Therefore arrived with the Jordanian party
  - US arm twisted Israel with the Billion Dollar Loan guarantee
  - So, Israel agreed
    - Condition → rescind UN resolution 3379
      - "determined that Zionism is a form of racism and racial discrimination”
      - revoked in 1991 General Assembly

## Consequence

- Paved the way for:
- Israel-Palestinian agreement: Oslo Accords
- Israel-Jordan peace treaty

# Oslo Accords: 1993

![[_attachments/image 2 2.jpeg|image 2 2.jpeg]]

- Secret process → No one other than Rabin, Arafat and their closest confidantes knew about the process
- Two protocols:
  - Exchange of letters btw Rabin and Arafat
    - Rabin →
      - they will consider PLO as representatives of Palestine
      - will begin negotiations
  - DoP: Declaration of Principles
- Only five people in the Palestinian delegation → none of them familiar with international law or English as a language
  - In contrast → Israeli delegation comprised of 14-15 people in which 7-8 members knew international law and had three drafts ready

## DoP

1. Aim → establish Palestinian ==interim== self govt authority (==Palestinian authority== - PA) by way of elections
   - PA → transitory authority for five years leading to a permanent settlement
   - Gives a window of five years in which the elected PA will assume charge of Palestinian affairs
2. ==Phased withdrawal== of Israeli forces (IDF) and subsequent assumption authority by PA
   - Start from Gaza and Jericho (West Bank)
   - Jericho → oldest living city in the world
   - should take place in the first three months
3. No discussion on ==Permanent status issues==
   - If these issues were not taken up → create trust → could be addressed at the end of five years
   - They were so controversial that if they were taken up, no consensus would be reached
   - Sixth year → permanent status issues would be addressed

### Afterthought

- Asked the parties to desist from doing anything that might prejudice these permanent status issues

### Motivation

- Placed heavy premium on good faith of both parties
- Envisaged a step-by-step process
  - End of five years, the parties would cut a peace treaty with each other

## Implementation (practice)

- Too difficult; too problematic
- Started with a difference of opinion on ==territorial extent== of Jericho
  - Israelis → Jericho town
  - Palestinians → Jericho town and vicinity
- ==Title== with which Arafat would be referred to
  - Palestinians → “President Arafat”
  - Israelis → “Chairman Arafat”
  - Compromise → “Raees Arafat” → “Raees” is an Arabic term can mean President, chairman, leader etc
  - Wasted 7-8 months
- “Asked the parties to desist from doing anything that might prejudice these permanent status issues” → no ==mechanism== to keep in check
  - Israel was expanding territories

## Permanent status issues

Fundamental points of contention of Israeli-Palestinian conflict

- Jerusalem
- Palestinian refugee crisis
- borders
- Israeli settlements

# Oslo II Accord - 1995

- Interim Agreement on the West Bank and the Gaza Strip
- Establishment of a Palestinian interim self-government in the Palestinian territories

## West Bank administrative divisions

- West Bank was divided → ==Areas A, B and C==
  - Areas A (important Palestinian towns | ~3%) → direct ==Palestinian control==
  - Area B (smaller Palestinian towns | ~37%) → Palestinian control but ==security, law & order== functions under Israeli authority
  - Area C (rural areas | ~70%) → direct ==Israeli== control
    - Included Israeli settlements
    - Included areas earmarked for huge Israeli infrastructure projects
- Gradual phase-by-phase handing over to PA was planned

## Problems

- No mechanism to check Israeli settlement activities
- This phase of complete Israeli withdrawal → never happened

## Aftermath

- Opposition from Israel right wing → Rabin assassinated
  - by Yigal Amir → Israeli ultranationalist
  - for “compromising too much”
- 1996 elections → Benjamin Netanyahu of the Likud party
  - always opposed to Oslo
  - Likud is the right wing

# 2000: Camp David II

![[_attachments/image 3 2.jpeg|image 3 2.jpeg]]

- between United States president ==Bill Clinton==, Israeli prime minister ==Ehud Barak== and Palestinian Authority chairman ==Yasser Arafat==

## Background

- 1999 → Netanyahu failed in elections → ==Labour party== won → ==Ehud Barak== became PM
  - campaign promise → negotiating peace treaty
- opportunity seized by Bill Clinton → initiated Camp David II
  - strange because he was a ==lame duck== president
  - the leaders knew that Clinton wouldn’t be there to see it through

![[_attachments/image 4 2.jpeg|image 4 2.jpeg]]

## Offer

- “Generous offer” → 90-95% of occupied territories
  - Barak’s offer was historic → no Israeli leader before him has made such an offer
  - Palestinians were reluctant
    - Oslo was not fulfilled ← withdrawal process was not complete (only ~7%)
- The precondition for discussion regarding permanent status issues should start after complete withdrawal ← Oslo
  - Here, Israeli withdrawal became the Israeli condition for Palestinian cessation
- Take it or leave it offer
  - Unfair because the negotiating offer itself could not be negotiated
- Prez Clinton offer → no one would be blamed if the negotiation failed
  - But he departed from this offer and blamed Arafat entirely

### Permanent status issues

- Territories → closely linked with settlements
  - Always a central question → how much territory Palestinians should have
  - Palestinian demand → pre-67 line
  - Generous offer was unfair → settlements
    - Israelis were not ready to move Israeli settlements
    - counter offer → land swap
      - all densely populated Israeli settlements will be under Israel sovereignty
      - But the exact percentage of land would be handed over to the Palestinians in the Souther part → completely unacceptable
    - This would lead to a disjoint state
      - already disjointed → West Bank and gaza

- Jerusalem
  - Main issue was Temple Mount / Haram-al-Sharif
  - West wall
    - Israel argues this is a remnant of the \_\_\_ that was built by King David → Jews pray there every Saturday and weep → Wailing wall
  - Palestinians argue that Temple Mount is in East Jerusalem, it should come under their sovereignty
    - Israel was not ready to cede any real control to Palestinians
    - Led to an array of creative proposals
      - One proposal was Palestinians can have custodial sovereignty and residual sovereignty for Israelis
      - Another proposal → Palestinians can have vertical sovereignty
        - they can have sovereignty of everything above the ground; below the ground → Israeli sovereignty
        - analogy: as if someone saying you can have your graveyard but you cannot bury the dead because the land below does not belong to you
