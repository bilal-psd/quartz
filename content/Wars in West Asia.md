# 1956: Suez Canal crisis

- “Second Arab–Israeli war” / “Tripartite Aggression”

- 1953: Gamal Abdel Nasser comes to power ← Free officers movement

![[_attachments/Untitled 11.png|Untitled 11.png]]

- 1956: World Bank cancels the loan
- In this backdrop → Nasser takes the drastic step → ==Nationalisation of Suez Canal==
- Ruffled the interest of UK and France
  - ==Suez Canal Company== ← consortium that controlled the Canal in which UK and France had a major stake
- UK & France joined with Israel and conceived a plan
  - Israel will capture the Suez Canal
  - France and UK will offer assistance on ground as well as in UN
  - “==Operation Musketeer==”
  - October 1956 → plan panned out
    - Within a week → entire Suez Canal was controlled by Israel

## Consequence

- Political debacle
- US was in a bind ← Two interventions happened in 1956
  - US was not informed
  - USSR invaded Hungary
  - Israel was assisted by France and UK
  - How does US oppose one intervention while supporting the other?
  - How do you impose sanctions on aggressors who are your allies? (and permanent members of the Security Council)
  - “Uniting for Peace” ← UN provision
    - If Security Council is deadlocked in an issue → General Assembly can take decision ← if Security Council had no issues
    - The two superpowers wanted this to be passed
- First instance where US arm twisted Israel to fall in line (vacate the Suez Canal)
- UNEF (United Nations Emergency Force) → Acted as a buffer
- Nasser became one of the most dominant leaders in the world ← stood up to the combined force of the West

# 1967: Six-day war

- “June war” / “1967 Arab–Israeli War” / “Third Arab–Israeli War”
- 5 to 10 June 1967
- between ==Israel and a coalition of Arab states== primarily comprising Jordan, Syria and Egypt

## Causes

- Expulsion of UNEF
- Closing of Straits of Tiran
- Egyptian military deployment along Sinai

### Suez Crisis

- 1956 Suez crisis → Israel’s goal was to reopen ==Straits of Tiran== ← closed by Egypt for all Israeli shipping since 1950
  - narrow sea passages between the Sinai and Arabian peninsulas
  - separate the ==Gulf of Aqaba== from the Red Sea proper
  - Access to Israel's only Red Sea seaport of Eilat is through the Gulf of Aqaba, which gives the Straits of Tiran strategic importance
    - Israel → vital interest as it is where Israel received vital imports, mainly oil from Iran

![[_attachments/Untitled 2 2.png|Untitled 2 2.png]]

- Israel → forced to withdraw → but guaranteed straits would remain open

## Events

- Israel reiterated its post-1956 position → another closure of the Straits of Tiran → ==casus belli== (situation that provokes war)

- May:
  - Nasser ordered expulsion of UNEF
  - Egypt closed Straits of Tiran to Israeli vessels
    - Egyptian military along Israel border
    - kicked out UNEF

![[_attachments/image.jpeg]]

- 5 June: Israel → ==pre-emptive airstrikes== on Egyptian and Syrian airfields
  - nearly the ==entire Egyptian Air Force== was destroyed
  - Israel → air supremacy advantage

![[_attachments/image 1.jpeg]]

- Israel military → ground offensive → Gaza Strip (Egyptian controlled) and Sinai Peninsula
  - Nasser ordered evacuation of Sinai peninsula
  - Israel → Conquered Sinai peninsula on the 6th day

![[_attachments/image 2.jpeg]]

- Egypt and Jordan agreed to a ceasefire on 8 June, and Syria agreed on 9 June; a ceasefire was signed with Israel on 11 June

## Consequence

1. ==Arab Humiliation==

   ![[_attachments/Untitled 3 2.png|Untitled 3 2.png]]

   - Israel had ==crippled== the entirety of the Egyptian, Syrian and Jordanian militaries
     - 20,000 Arab troops killed while Israel lost fewer than 1,000 of its own
   - humiliated Egypt, Jordan, and Syria, and led Nasser to resign in shame
     - Nasser later reinstated following protests
   - Israel captured territories:
     - Golan Heights from Syria
     - the ==West Bank== and East Jerusalem from Jordan
     - ==Gaza Strip== as well as the entire Sinai Peninsula from Egypt
2. ==Strategic depth== table was turned

   ![[_attachments/image 3.jpeg]]

   - Greater the distance between a country’s frontiers and mainland → Larger the ability to sustain enemy attack
   - Gives the country a depth to which it can allow enemy to enter its territory
     - Once the supply line thins or weather becomes unfavourable → you can attack
   - Israel → Strategic depth increased (previously: none)
   - Jordan → Amman was brought closer to Israel
   - Syria → Damascus can be seen from Israel
   - Egypt → Decreased strategic depth
3. Radicalisation of ==PLO==

   ![[_attachments/image 4.jpeg]]

   - Legally created by Arab League
     - Actually ==Nasser== who played a significant role
   - Created to manage Palestinian nationalist aspirations, not to articulate it
     - One variant of the movement that was extremist → ==Fatah== → armed struggle
     - ==Yasser Arafat== and his two colleagues created Arafat
   - 1964 - 67 it was moderate; Fatah was underground
   - After 1967 → Fatah was asked to take the mantle of PLO
     - 1967 to his death → Arafat was the Chairman
4. Israeli ==settlements==
   - Initial → ==military settlements== → impose law and order
   - Gradually, ==civilian settlements== started to be formed
   - Contemporary International Law: Geneva convention → The occupying country has to:
     - Impose law and order in newly acquired territory
     - Not supposed to transfer civilian settlements
   - ==Begin doctrine==
     - Israel’s response → not ‘illegal’ occupation
   - 1948 war → Palestinian refugee crisis
   - 1967 war → Settlement conflict
5. ==UN resolution 242==
   - Acquiring territory by war is inadmissible
   - ==Withdrawal== of Israeli military from recently occupied territory
     - ==Extent== of withdrawal not specified → ambiguity
   - Respect for ==sovereignty==, territorial integrity and political independence of other states and their right to live free of threat
     - No mention of Palestinians → referred to as ‘the refugee problem’
   - Two interpretations
     - Arab states: stressed on withdrawal of Israeli military
     - Israel: stressed on respect for sovereignty
6. Entry of ==religion== into politics
   - Religious understanding of political events
   - For Israel → ==Divine intervention==
     - Defeating the combined strength of three armies in a matter of 6 days
     - ‘Creation of world in ==6 days== and resting on 7th’ → analogy was made
   - Political earthquake in Israel
     - Till 1967 → dominance of the ==Labour party== → comes to an end in 1977
       - Labour Party : Centre-left
     - ==Likud Party== won elections in 1977 (Menachem Begin)

       ![[_attachments/image 5.jpeg]]

       - Conservative, Centre-right
   - Affected the Arabs
     - Defeated because they deviated from the ==path of Islam==
       - accepting foreign ideals (nationalism, communism etc)
     - Course correction → ‘==political Islam==’
   - Christian reading
     - Biblical prophecy of ==Second coming of Jesus==→ Jerusalem has to be under the control of Jews

# 1973: October war / Yom Kippur war

- Ramadan War / 1973 Arab–Israeli War / Fourth Arab–Israeli War
  - Two Arab countries only → Egypt and Syria

- Change in leadership in Egypt
  - Nasser death → followed by ==Anwar Sadat==

![[_attachments/image 6.jpeg]]

## Causes

- Sadat → wanted to break the status quo
  - ==surprise attack== on Israel ← ‘Six day war’ is understood to be a surprise war from Israel
  - Later found out to be not so surprise

- Failure of Mossad ← ==Complacency== of Israel ← idea that Arab countries will not initiate war with Israel unless they had long distance missiles (==Scud== missiles)
  - Early 70s → Cold War ‘==detente==’ between the two superpowers → assumed that Soviets will not supply the hardware to Arabs
  - Major miscalculation → Arabs will accept the status quo

![[_attachments/image 7.jpeg]]

## Events

- ==October 6==, 1973 (Jewish holy day of Yom Kippur; also Ramadan) → surprise attack

- Started with the successful crossing of Suez Canal by the Egyptian army - ==Operation Badr==
  - In conjunction with Syrian attack on Golan Heights

![[_attachments/image 8.jpeg]]

- Syrian and Egyptian forces came dangerously close to Israel proper
  - Repelled by the help provided by the US

![[_attachments/image 9.jpeg]]

- After hostilities started → ==US and USSR== initiated massive resupply efforts to their respective allies → led to a ==near-confrontation== between the two nuclear-armed superpowers
  - US airlifted weapons and supplies to Israel → ==Operation Nickel Grass==

![[_attachments/image 10.jpeg]]

Operation Nickel Grass

- Counterattack by Israel → turned the tide in their favour → stalemate

- October 17 → OPEC reduced oil production by 5% and imposed ==embargo== on US
  - Major price hikes around the world

![[_attachments/image 11.jpeg]]

Gas station converted to revival hall during crisis

- Lasted ==twenty days==
- 22 October → Initial ceasefire
- 25 October → Second ceasefire; End of war

### Significance

- US
  - Initially →Americans unwilling to provide hardware to Israeli due to the detente
  - Kissinger (who had argued not to intervene) → after the attack → American hardware airlifted to Israel
- Superpowers came dangerously close to an open confrontation

### Consequence

- Crack in the foundational myth (national consensus) of Israel
  - ‘Arabs cannot be partners in peace’
    - No negotiation possible
    - Solidified 1948 onwards
    - Crack → Kissinger enforcing ceasefire between three parties → ‘==Shuttle diplomacy==’
      - Culminated in the ==First Egypt-Israel peace treaty== (==Camp David I==)

![[_attachments/image 12.jpeg]]

- First Oil Shock
  - OPEC (under the leadership of Saudi Arabia) → placed an embargo on Israel and all western allies (including US and Europe) → First Oil Shock of 1973-74
  - Impact on Arabs → Arab image war tarnished
    - “They are ‘irrational’ and will go to any extreme including creating an energy crisis”
    - “Israel is the only ‘rational’ presence in the region”
    - Embargo really hurt the Western World
      - ==US never officially opposed the embargo. Why?==

## 1987: First Intifada

- Civil disobedience movement
- Happened in occupied territories
- Dec 1987 - Sept 1993
  - Ended with the Oslo accords
- Very well planned
- Despite resorting to force → IDF could not succeed in quelling this movement

### Cause

- Short term cause
  - IDF truck killed Palestinian civilians
    - Israel → accident
    - Palestinians → intentional
    - Incident which broke the Intifada
- Long term cause
  - Continued occupation of Gaza and West Bank and its negative impacts

### Significance

- Involved people from all walks of life
  - All ages → children(13yo) to old people(70yo)
- Took both IDF and PLO by surprise
  - IDF: Entire civilian population was rising against up them
    - Up until now, they were facing guerrilla warfare from PLO
    - Mass movement
  - PLO: Paradox
    - Leadership was clueless on what was happening on the ground
    - Relocated to Tunisia → great distance between the Intifada and PLO
    - people provided the agenda for PLO to work with
    - PLO came to accept two of the demands
      - Recognised the two-state solution
        - tacit acknowledgement of the existence of Israel
      - Rescind the idea of armed struggle
- Israel → Political solution became desirable
  - Road to Oslo Accords was paved

### Consequences

- Israel → Increasing realisation that political solution was needed
- PLO → Need to accept Israel and reject armed struggle
- Hamas?
