# Cold war

![[_attachments/Untitled 13.png|Untitled 13.png]]

- Why cold? Rivalry between superpowers → but not direct → waged war through proxies
- Closest that they came to a direct conflict → ==Cuban missile crisis==
  - Soviets placed missiles in Cuba ← in response to American deployment of missiles in Italy and Turkey

    ![[_attachments/Untitled 1 4.png|Untitled 1 4.png]]

  - Negotiations went on for 13 days
- Goal → acquire as many allies as possible
- There can never be a balance between two powers → always need a balancer (a third party)
  - eg: France vs Germany → Britain was balancer
  - Cold War → no balancing power → ==Balancer was Nuclear weapons==

## Mutual Assured Destruction (MAD)

- ==Second strike capability:== capability to sustain first strike and deploy second strike
- If Nuclear was somehow initiated → Destruction of both parties was assured

![[_attachments/Untitled 2 4.png|Untitled 2 4.png]]

## Unique features of Cold War:

1. Presence of Nuclear weapons → MAD
2. Role of ==ideology== → ==Liberal capitalism vs Communism/Socialism==
   - Clash of two ideals → ==Liberty vs Equality==
   - Because of ideology → Reagan could describe USSR as ‘evil’
   - Rivalry bereft of ideology → Coexistence is possible
   - Rivalry with ideology → ~~Coexistence~~ → Either you are right or wrong → Survival is at stake → Law, human rights were inconsequential

## Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)

- 120 developing countries → not formally aligned with or against any major power bloc
- remain neutral in this Cold War between these two superpowers
- US response → neutrality is in an ‘immoral’ position
  - because survival is at stake (because of ideological rivalry)
- Initiative of Jawaharlal Nehru, Gamal Abdel Nasser and Josip Broz Tito (Yugoslav president)

# US presidential doctrines and West Asia

### Monroe Doctrine

- James Monroe - 1823
- European powers → keep away from Latin America

![[_attachments/image.png]]

### Truman Doctrine

- ==Financial aid== to countries that were threatened by the communist government, thereby offering assistance to those countries resisting communism
- Announced in 1947 to ‘contain’ the communist threat posed by the USSR
- Enunciated as an American response vis-a-vis ==civil war in Turkey and Greece==
- Two things that Truman did for Europe
  - **Marshall plan:** Plan for ==economic recovery== of Western Europe → foreign aid / recovery programmes
  - **NATO:**
    - Created specifically to counter the Soviet threat
    - ==Military== alliance
    - In response → ==Warsaw pact== (Soviet Union + Eastern Bloc)

![[_attachments/image 1.png]]

### Eisenhower Doctrine

- a ==Middle Eastern country== could request American economic or military assistance if it was being threatened by communist aggression
- An addition to Truman doctrine → ==Military aid==
- **Baghdad pact:**
  - formed in 1955 by Iran, Iraq, Pakistan, Turkey and the United Kingdom
  - 1958 nationalist coup in Iraq → Iraq withdrew → renamed as CENTO (Central Treaty Organisation)
    - Least influential alliance

![[_attachments/image 2.png]]

### Nixon Doctrine

- Announced in 1969 to fine-tune American response in managing the region
- Nixon stated that "the United States would assist in the defense and developments of allies and friends", but would not "undertake all the defense of the free nations of the world.”
- each ally nation was in charge of its own security in general, but the United States would act as a nuclear shield when requested
- Only two conditions when US would intervene:
  - When it can make a real difference
  - Considered in their own interest

![[_attachments/image 3.png]]

- ‘Twin pillar’ policy
  - Two client states (allies) - Iran and Saudi Arabia
  - US sought to ensure stability through cooperation with the allies
- This doctrine fell flat in 1979
  - Islamic revolution in Iran
  - Soviet invasion of Afghanistan
- Reasoning behind this doctrine
  - campaign promise of Nixon → will bring back “boys” from Vietnam
  - ‘Vietnam syndrome’ → You should only intervene in territories which you know and where there is an easy exit ← Vietnam exemplified this

### Brezhnev doctrine - Soviet Union

- Mirror image
- Soviet Union will come to the help of any socialist country under threat

## Consequence of Cold War

- Alliances
  - All the monarchies → aligned with US
  - All republics → aligned with USSR
- Failure of doctrines
  - Failed to account for the emerging Nationalism in West Asia
  - Basic idea of threat (from Truman onwards) was the communist threat
    - But for Arabs → clear and present threat was Israel
