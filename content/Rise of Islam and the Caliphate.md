# West Asia

![[_attachments/Screenshot_2022-01-25_at_10.07.00_AM.png]]

## **Cultural significance of West Asia:**

- Birthplace of three great Abrahamic religions: Judaism, Christianity, and Islam
- The commonality of these religions: monotheistic: Judaism was the first to conceptualise as such

### Jerusalem

Jerusalem was especially significant for all three religions

- For Jews:
  - It was the capital of the ‘promised land’ - Biblical claim
  - Masjid al Aqsa built on top of Temple Mount
  - Jews claim this temple was constructed by David and finished by Solomon
  - ‘Western wall’ claimed to be a remnant of the original temple by David
- For Christians:
  - Jesus was born in Bethlehem and crucified in the outskirts of Jerusalem
  - Witnessed the rise and fall of Jesus Christ
- For Muslims:
  - Al-Mi’raj (Prophet Muhammed was called by God to go to Jerusalem from Mecca (Al-Isra) and from Jerusalem to the eighth tier of heaven where he met God) happened here
  - There was a time when Muslims used to pray facing Jerusalem
- Jerusalem has seen bloody massacres and bloody wars because of its significance to all three religions

## Emergence of Islam

- During the emergence of Islam, West Asia was surrounded by two great empires: the Byzantine empire in Turkey and the Sassanid empire in Persia
- Time before the emergence of Islam was called Jahiliyya (Age of Ignorance) in Islam

# Islam

## Features of Arabs

- Arabs take unusual pride in:
  - Language: Like every other culture, Arabs have a concept of ‘us vs them’
    - ‘Arabi vs Ajami’ - ‘person who speaks’ vs ‘person who cannot speak’
  - Memory
    - Hafiz - someone who has memorised the Qur’an
- Desert conditions played a huge role in the strictness of Islam
- Vendetta used to run for generations for Arabs

## Features of Islam

- Equality
- No distinction between Arabi and Ajam
  - Mohammed highlighted this in his last speech
- Only destination is based on Taqwa (piety)
- No elaborate rituals, priestly class

### Five pillars of Islam

- Shahada - profession of faith
- Salat - prayer
- Zakat - alms
  - mandatory, not charity
  - 2.5%
- Sawm - fasting
  - In the month of Ramadan
- Hajj - pilgrimage

## Prophet Mohammed

- Story of Islam is connected very closely with the story of Prophet Mohammed
- Timeline: 570 - 632 CE
- Meditated in Hira at 40yo
  - The first person to convert was Khadijah, wife of Mohammed
- Hijrat - 622 CE
  - Migration from Makkah to Medina
  - Start of Islamic Ummah
  - so significant that Islamic calendar starts here
- Several battles between Muslims and people of Makkah
- The capture of Makkah - 630 CE
  - Smashed all the idols inside Ka’bah
  - Pardoned the Makkans, despite the popular opinion being to put them to death
- Died in 632 CE
- He was both a spiritual/religious as well as a political leader. Therefore, panic when he died

# Mongols

![[_attachments/Untitled 4 2.png|Untitled 4 2.png]]

- Mongols were a nomadic tribe → consolidated by Genghis Khan
  - Genghis Khan used to say he was a curse descended upon the world by the Gods (”The scourge of God”)
- They used to raze everything to the ground in wars
  - They give an ultimatum → if fails, then huge massacres was performed
  - They had no use to retain agricultural land since they were a nomadic tribe
- Mongol empire was the largest empire the world had ever seen → surpassed only by the British Empire
- Grandson of Genghis Khan - Hulagu Khan razed Baghdad
  - Bayt-al-Hikma (House of wisdom) - largest library in the world - was destroyed
  - After besieging Baghdad, they converted to Islam
- Mongol invasion was very significant
  - Put to end the Abbasid rule
  - Led to the ending of the institution of Ijtihad
- Process of fragmentation

### Ijtihad

- Mohammed used to arbitrate any conflicts on religion / politics / ethics
- After his death, the institution of Ijtihad came up
- Ijtihad → consensus of the Ummah
- Very authoritative; but manifested the open-mindedness of the Muslim society
  - Came in handy when there was ambiguity
  - When things weren't clear from Hadeeth or Quran
- Ijtihad was closed after the Mongol invasion
  - Effects felt until now → Muslim societies are ‘closed’
  - Revival of Ijtihad would bring reform to Contemporary muslim societies as they would be able to come template on contemporary issues better

# Ottoman empire

![[_attachments/Untitled 5 2.png|Untitled 5 2.png]]

- Official term was Usmaniya empire
- Established by Osman I
- Humble beginnings → went onto consolidate heart of Middle East + Balkans and laid siege twice to Vienna
- 1453 - Fall of Constantinople (Istanbul) - capital of the Byzantine empire
  - End of Byzantine empire (1000 years of Byzantine era)
  - Hagia Sophia: church → museum → mosque (reverted according to the Turkish state)
- One of the major reasons of demonisation of Islam is intertwined with the rise of Ottomans
  - ‘Enemy at the gate’

### Two institutions:

- Devshirme - ‘child levy’ / ‘blood tax’
  - recruiting soldiers / bureaucrats from Christian families
  - forced to convert Islam
  - soldiers: ‘Janissaries’
  - lived in barracks, separate from civil population
  - owe their allegiance only to the Sultan
  - no land, marriage rights
  - counterbalance the Turkish aristocratic class / nobility
- Millet
  - The Millet System refers to **the Ottoman administration of separate religious communities** that acknowledged each community's authority in overseeing its own communal affairs, primarily through independent religious court systems and schools
  - condition was to accept the suzerainty of the Sultan
  - gave the required stability to the Ottoman empire

### Mid-19th century → weakening of Ottoman empire

- Internal
  - Only when cracks appeared to these institutions → decline of Ottoman empire
  - Janissaries (mid-19th century) → started owning land and marrying
- External
